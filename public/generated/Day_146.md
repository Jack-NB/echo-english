In a small agricultural town, the mayor decided to hold a grand feast to celebrate the autumn harvest. The main dish was a giant baked **potato** (马铃薯), grown with pride by local farmers. But the mayor warned everyone: "Do not **pretend** (假装) to enjoy it if you don't, lest (以免) we waste food." As guests began to **dine** (进餐), a linguistics professor stood up to correct a child's **pronunciation** (发音) of the word "potato," which caused laughter. The professor then explained that sugar would quickly **dissolve** (溶解) in the hot butter, and began to **speculate** (推测) about the ancient origins of the crop.

He noted that the **underlying** (根本的) cultural history of the potato **preceded** (先于) the modern era, and that each family had a **quota** (配额) of potatoes to share. After the meal, there was a **surplus** (盈余、过剩) of food, so the mayor proposed a **drastic** (激烈的) plan: to donate the leftovers to the neighboring town. He asked a senior official to **supervise** (监督) the delivery, reminding him that he was **liable** (有义务的) for any mistake. The local **senator** (参议员) arrived late and promised to **abolish** (废除) the outdated food tax, which he could **foresee** (预见) would cause **hardship** (艰难) for poor families. Meanwhile, scientists discussed how **hormones** (激素) in livestock affected growth, and the professor was the main **recipient** (接受者) of a grant to study this.

Every word they spoke was recorded, and the professor smiled, knowing that the **preceding** (前述的) conversation had created a **precedent** (先例) for future community meetings. The **supervisor** (主管) wrote a **receipt** (收据) for the donated food, and the **senator** praised the **foresight** (远见) of the mayor. In the end, the potato feast became a legend, remembered not for its **pronounced** (显著的) flavors, but for the **unpretentious** (谦逊的) spirit of the town.
