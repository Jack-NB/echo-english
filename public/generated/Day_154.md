**连锁故事 (英文)**

In a **gloomy**(阴暗的) old mansion, a **lazy**(懒惰的) **painter**(画家) named Leo sat over a half-finished canvas. He knew that a **feasible**(可行的) plan was to **obey**(遵守、服从) his client’s **hint**(暗示) and use brighter colors, but he felt too tired to **stir**(搅拌) his paint. His **bitter**(苦的) coffee had gone cold, and the **butter**(黄油) on his toast had melted into a **yellow**(黄色) puddle—everything seemed to **digest**(消化) into a dull routine.

Suddenly, a **civilian**(平民) from the village rushed in, holding a **pin**(别针) that had been used to **fix** a torn **carpet**(地毯). “Please,” she begged, “help me **retrieve**(取回、恢复) my lost ring from the well!” Leo sighed. He was **illiterate**(文盲) in practical matters, but his **iron**(熨) will was strong. He grabbed a **pie**(馅饼) from the table, broke it into crumbs, and dropped them into the well—the fish would be tempted, and the ring would surface. The woman laughed, touching her **lip**(嘴唇) in disbelief.

As the sun broke through the **exotic**(奇异的、外来的) trees outside, Leo realized that even a lazy artist could create a grand **painting**(绘画) of life. He picked up his brush, **stirring**(搅拌的) the **gloom**(忧郁) away, and began to paint the **feasibility**(可行性) of hope—a **bitterly**(苦涩地) sweet scene of **civilization**(文明) and **obedience**(顺从) to one’s own heart.
