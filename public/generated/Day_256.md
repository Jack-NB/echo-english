In the old **auditorium** (礼堂), the stage manager’s **repertoire** (全部节目) was famous for its wild mix. Tonight, the first act featured a **rose** (玫瑰) ballet, where dancers twirled in a **spiral** (螺旋) up the **staircase** (楼梯). A huge **triangle** (三角形) prop hung from above, **adjacent** (邻近的) to a broken **antenna** (天线) that **approximated** (接近) a piece of art. The **arena** (竞技场) floor was cracked like a dried **artery** (动脉), and the lighting crew had to **bandage** (绷带) the cables with **bark** (树皮) from a nearby tree. The director, who always demanded the **best** (最、最好的), consulted a dusty **bibliography** (参考书目) to find a **blossom** (花) metaphor for the finale. Meanwhile, a **camel** (骆驼) was led on stage, carrying a fake **cannon** (大炮) made of cardboard, and the prop master placed a **capsule** (胶囊) containing a note inside the barrel. As the **autumn** (秋天) leaves drifted through the open roof, the audience gasped. The show was a chaotic masterpiece—every **derivative** (派生词) like **audibility** (可听性), **proximity** (接近), and **encapsulation** (封装) seemed to come alive. The **arterial** (动脉的) rhythm of the drums pulsed, and the **blossoming** (开花) finale made everyone forget the **cannonade** (炮击) of technical errors. It was, without doubt, their **best** (最好的) night ever.
