**The Fisherman’s Ingenious Descent**

The old **fisherman** (渔夫) felt an urgent **descent** (下降) of energy as he prepared for his final **excursion** (远足) of the season. He packed his favorite **rod** (杆) and a small **fax** (传真) machine, a ridiculous gadget his son insisted he carry for emergencies. After a hearty **dessert** (甜点) of honey cakes, he set sail with the morning **fleet** (舰队). The sea was calm, but a sudden storm began to **haul** (拖、拉) his boat sideways. He tried to **rub** (擦) the salt spray from his eyes, but the waves threatened to **smash** (粉碎、打碎) his hull. Just then, a **graphic** (图表) on his son’s faxed weather report warned of a **hell** (地狱) of a squall. He had to **expel** (驱逐) all fear and steer toward a cove. His **ingenious** (精巧的、有独创性的) plan? Use a heavy anchor **rod** as a lever to shift the boat’s weight. The **repetition** (重复) of crashing waves was relentless, but he finally anchored. Exhausted, he began a quiet **meditation** (冥想) on the sea’s power. He realized that every **script** (脚本) of survival is written by trial and error. He unloaded his **merchandise** (商品) of fish, which the storm had miraculously preserved. He thought of how the rich often **oppress** (压迫) the poor, but here, nature was the only master. As he kissed (亲吻) the shore, he whispered a thanks to his son’s **fleet** (舰队) of gadgets. The old man smiled — no captain could ever **expel** (驱逐) the ocean’s mystery, but one could learn to **descent** (下降) into humility. That night, he dreamed of a **kiss** (亲吻) from the sea itself, a gentle farewell.
