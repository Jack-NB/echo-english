Leo had always loved **sport** (体育运动), especially mechanics, which he believed was a kind of mental **sport** — solving puzzles. “If a problem doesn’t **exist** (存在), why worry?” he laughed. His main **advantage** (优势) was patience. He read the **newspaper** (报纸) every morning to learn new techniques, and he could **remember** (记得) every diagram he saw. His father didn’t fully **agree** (同意) with his hobby, but Leo, now a **manager** (经理) at a small garage, knew that **land** (着陆、土地) safely was the first rule of any repair. He had to **solve** (解答) the oil leak, and every part had a **function** (功能). The **management** (经营、管理) of time was crucial. He repeated a simple **phrase** (短语) to himself: “Test, then trust.” So he ran a small **experiment** (实验) with a new seal, checking the **structure** (结构) of the engine block. Finally, the car started smoothly. Leo smiled — **application** (应用) of knowledge always paid off.
